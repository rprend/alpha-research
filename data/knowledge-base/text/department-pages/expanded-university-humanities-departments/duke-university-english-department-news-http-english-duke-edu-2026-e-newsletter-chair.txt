2026 e-Newsletter Chair | English Department Skip to main content Enter your keywords Search Menu Secondary Menu News Events --> 2026 e-Newsletter Chair It was a year of great growth for the English department, as we welcomed three new permanent faculty members: Professor Richard So, who specializes in computational literary studies and digital humanities; Professor Marguerite Nguyen, who specializes in Asian American Literature; and Professor Timothy Heimlich, who specializes in eighteenth-century and Romantic-era British literature. Their courses and expertise have already added so much to our curriculum, and we look forward to everything they will bring us in coming years! The novelist Camille Bordas also joined us for the year as our third Blackburn Distinguished Artist-in-Residence. This year we also bid a bittersweet farewell to two treasured colleagues: Professor Robert Mitchell, who will be leaving Duke for Leuphana University in Germany; and Professor Victor Strandborg who is retiring after an astonishing 60 years in Duke’s English Department. Professor Strandberg’s farewell letter, included in this newsletter, is very much worth your time! As for conferences and events, we were busier than ever in 2025-26! The third annual Armstrong-Tennenhouse lecture was delivered by Professor Nathan Hensley, of Georgetown University (and a alumni of our graduate program!). We hosted the Triangle’s Undergraduate Research Symposium, organized by graduating senior Trisha Santanam, and the Post45 Grad Symposium, organized by PhD students Julia Gordon and Cassandra Luca. Professor Mesha Maren organized a broad-ranging symposium on “The Life of the Novel in a Post-Test World,” which brought together critics, publishers, reviewers, and creative writers for a rich discussion of the current literary ecosystem. A series of lunchtime presentations by our new faculty introduced us to their current work. As I come to the end of my four years as department chair, I am deeply grateful to have worked with such a vibrant, generous, and thoughtful community of faculty, students, and staff. But I know I leave you in good hands with the incoming chair, Professor Priscilla Wald!
