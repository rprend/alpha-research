# The First Cultural Observatory Field Map

This draft is generated from the first runnable metadata corpus. It should not be published as-is until the source backfill is expanded and the representative works are reviewed.

## Thesis

A public research site about cultural change should lead with inspectable findings, not with the label `quantitative humanities`.

## Corpus Note

The current corpus contains 1951 deduped records from hcommons, crossref, arxiv, doaj, openalex, osf-preprints. 1155 records are dated 2024-present.

## Findings To Develop

- 593 records currently show quantitative, computational, corpus, citation, AI, or method signals.
- The highest-count labels are digital humanities, literature, philosophy, history, sociology.
- The strongest near-term essay is a field map of AI and the humanities, followed by canon drift and institutional-language drift.

## Limitations

- This first artifact uses public metadata and abstracts where available, not full-text scholarship.
- Open indexes underrepresent books, chapters, non-English work, and paywalled humanities journals.
- Trend claims require source-coverage checks and manual review of representative works before publication.

## What To Study Next

- The Rise Of AI And The Humanities: The corpus already tracks AI, LLM, computational, and digital-humanities records across scholarly sources.
- Canon Drift: Canon, reception, citation, syllabi, and translation records can seed a broader corpus about cultural memory.
- What Changed In University Mission Statements?: This should be run as a separate longitudinal web corpus because it needs institution pages and historical snapshots, not only publication metadata.
