## Thesis

A humanities dataset becomes analytically useful when access, rights, metadata structure, time depth, and coverage bias are legible together.

## Corpus Note

The registry probes 15 candidate corpora. 11 were accessible, 6 were ready-now, 5 needed work, and 4 were blocked or constrained in the latest run. It generated 5 study specs.

## Ready-Now Examples

- Cleveland Museum of Art Open Access API (museum-collections, score 103): What acquisition, creation-date, and department patterns appear in open museum data? Risk: One museum collection is analytically rich but institution-specific.
- Wikidata (linked-open-data, score 96): How are authors, artworks, institutions, languages, and movements connected in public linked data? Risk: Wikidata reflects contributor attention and ontology gaps.
- Library of Congress Digital Collections (archives-library, score 93): Which subjects, formats, and eras dominate digitized public collections? Risk: Collection metadata reflects digitization and cataloging priorities.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art Collection API (museum-collections, score 93): How do object dates, geographies, departments, and acquisition histories differ across the Met collection? Risk: Collection API records vary in completeness and public-domain image availability.
- Open Syllabus (teaching-syllabi, score 86): Which works and authors appear in syllabi across institutions, fields, and time? Risk: Public access and export terms need explicit review before automated collection.
- Project Gutenberg (books-literature, score 86): How do genres, publication dates, and author cohorts shape public-domain literary corpora? Risk: Public-domain availability is not the same as literary representativeness.
