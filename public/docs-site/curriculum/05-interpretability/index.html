Phase 5 — Interpretability: opening the model up

Goal: read and replicate mechanistic interpretability ("mech interp") research — Anthropic's signature science. This is the field where a person with one GPU and good taste can still do publishable work, which makes it the most strategically important phase for your goal #1.

Time: ~4 weeks. Cost: <$20.

The question of the field: we have a function made of a trillion floats that writes poetry and code, and *nobody knows how it works* — not metaphorically; the people who built it cannot tell you the mechanism by which it added 17+25. Interp is reverse-engineering that mechanism. It's the closest thing in AI to being handed an alien artifact with a debugger attached.

5.1 The object of study: activations

Distinguish two kinds of arrays once and forever: weights (fixed after training; the model file) and activations (the intermediate (T, d_model) float arrays produced at every layer *during one forward pass on one input*; discarded immediately in normal use). Interp's raw material is activations: run text through a frozen model, *keep* the intermediates, study them. An "activation dataset" is literally: run 10M tokens through the model, dump layer-12 residual-stream vectors to disk → a (10M, 768) array.

The organizing picture (from Phase 1, now load-bearing): the residual stream is a per-token communication bus; every attention head and MLP reads from it and adds its output back. So the stream's vector at layer L is a *sum of contributions* — and interp's working hypothesis is that concepts ("features") are directions in this vector space, with model behavior decomposable into circuits: small subgraphs of heads/neurons implementing identifiable algorithms.

5.2 The toolkit (everything is array surgery)

Install TransformerLens: it loads open models with hooks — functions you register that get called during the forward pass with the activation array, and may *read or overwrite it in place*. Every technique below is a few lines of hook code:

  • Logit lens: take the residual stream at layer L (not the last layer), multiply by the unembedding matrix → see what the model "would predict so far." Watching the prediction sharpen layer-by-layer is the cheapest profound experiment in the field. (Origin: a 2020 *blog post* by nostalgebraist — note the venue; this field's canon is half blog posts.)
  • Linear probing: train logistic regression from activation vectors to a label ("is this token inside a Python string?"). High accuracy ⇒ the info is linearly present at that layer. Caution you'll see debated: probes show *presence*, not *use*.
  • Activation patching (causal intervention): run the model on input A ("The Eiffel Tower is in") and on B ("The Colosseum is in"); re-run A but use a hook to splice in B's activation at one site; if the output flips Paris→Rome, that site causally carries the fact. This is interp's randomized controlled trial, and the methodological backbone of the whole field.
  • Ablation: zero (or mean-replace) a head's output; measure the damage.
  • Steering: *add* a direction to the stream at inference ("Golden Gate Claude" was this, productionized as a stunt). Compute a direction as mean(activations on concept) − mean(activations off concept).

5.3 The findings canon (what we actually know)

  1. Induction heads (Anthropic 2022, *In-context Learning and Induction Heads*): a two-head circuit implementing "the sequence …A B … A should predict B" — copy-paste from context. They emerge in a sudden phase change during training, visible as a bump in the loss curve, and are a substrate of in-context learning. Fully understood, findable in *your* Phase-2 model.
  2. Superposition (*Toy Models of Superposition*, Anthropic 2022): models represent more features than they have dimensions by storing them as nearly-orthogonal directions, tolerating interference because features are sparse. Consequence: individual neurons are polysemantic (fire for unrelated things), which is *why* you can't read meaning off single neurons and why the field needed…
  3. Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) (*Towards Monosemanticity* 2023; *Scaling Monosemanticity* 2024 — the Claude 3 Sonnet features paper). What an SAE physically is: a one-hidden-layer autoencoder — encoder matrix (d_model, d_dict) with d_dict 8–64× wider, ReLU, decoder back — trained on your dumped activation dataset with loss = reconstruction error + λ·(sparsity penalty on the hidden layer). The sparse hidden units come out startlingly monosemantic: a unit for Golden Gate Bridge, one for code backdoors, one for sycophantic praise. The decoder rows are the feature directions. Training one takes a GPU-day at small scale.
  4. Circuit tracing / attribution graphs (*On the Biology of a Large Language Model*, Anthropic 2025): replace MLPs with interpretable feature layers, then trace which features cause which downstream features → readable computational graphs of real behaviors — multi-step recall ("Dallas → Texas → Austin"), poetry *planned ahead*, arithmetic done by parallel estimation, and unfaithful chain-of-thought (the stated reasoning demonstrably not matching the internal mechanism — read that section twice; it matters for safety arguments).
  5. Training-dynamics curiosities every paper assumes you know: grokking (arXiv:2201.02177 — generalization long *after* memorization, on toy tasks), double descent, lottery tickets. Skim-level.

5.4 The structured path: ARENA

ARENA (Alignment Research Engineer Accelerator) — exercise notebooks built by/for people who became Anthropic/DeepMind research engineers; chapters 0–1 cover exactly this phase (transformers from scratch → TransformerLens → induction heads → SAEs → the IOI circuit). Treat it as your problem-set book alongside this file. Complement: Neel Nanda's "Concrete Steps to Get Started in Mechanistic Interpretability" + his 200 Concrete Open Problems list — which doubles as a menu for Phase 6.

5.5 The build (replications — this phase's builds *are* research training)

  1. Find induction heads in your own model. Feed your Phase-2 GPT a sequence of random tokens repeated twice; per-token loss should crash on the second copy. Locate which heads, via per-head ablations. Bonus: find the phase-change bump in your saved Phase-2 loss curve.
  2. Replicate Toy Models of Superposition — small enough for a laptop notebook; reproduce the pentagon-geometry figure. You'll never forget what superposition means after watching features arrange themselves.
  3. Train an SAE on layer ~8 of your model (or GPT-2-small) from a few hundred M tokens of dumped activations; build a minimal feature dashboard (top-activating contexts per feature); find 5 clean features and 5 messy ones; write up what λ does to the tradeoff.
  4. One patching study: replicate a slice of the IOI circuit ("Indirect Object Identification", arXiv:2211.00593 — the field's model-organism paper) in GPT-2-small via ARENA's notebook.

5.6 Now readable

In order: A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits (Anthropic 2021 — the residual-stream formalism; hard, foundational, worth two passes) → Induction Heads (2022) → Toy Models of Superposition (2022) → Towards Monosemanticity (2023) → Scaling Monosemanticity (2024) → On the Biology of an LLM (2025). All at transformer-circuits.pub. Plus IOI (2211.00593), grokking (2201.02177), and — for the skeptical-methodology muscle — *Interpretability Illusions*-genre critiques and the probing caveats above.

Checkpoint

  1. Weights vs. activations: which does an SAE train on? What exactly is its training set, as a file?
  2. Why does superposition make neuron-level interpretation hopeless, and how does the SAE's sparsity penalty counteract it?
  3. Design (on paper) a patching experiment to test whether a model computes "capital of the state containing Dallas" via an intermediate "Texas" representation.
  4. Your induction-head hunt: which layer.head indices? What was the loss on copy-1 vs copy-2?
  5. Explain to a smart friend why "the chain-of-thought may not reflect the actual computation" — and why that matters.
