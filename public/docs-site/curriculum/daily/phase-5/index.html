Phase 5 daily packets — Interpretability (Days 96–119)

This phase ends with you having replicated five published mech-interp results on real models — logit lens, induction heads, superposition, an SAE, and the IOI circuit — and read the transformer-circuits canon. Prerequisite: Phase 4 checkpoint passed.

Day 96 — TransformerLens setup + logit lens

⏱ ~75 min · Link: TransformerLens

Do: pip install transformer_lens, load GPT-2-small via HookedTransformer.from_pretrained, and run a few prompts with run_with_cache. Implement the logit lens: at each layer, take resid_post, apply final LayerNorm and the unembedding W_U, and plot the correct next token's probability layer by layer (background: section 5.2 of the phase guide; the technique comes from a 2020 nostalgebraist blog post — half this field's canon is blog posts).

Explains: Activations vs. weights — the residual stream is a per-token vector you can decode at any depth, and the prediction sharpens layer by layer.

Done when: A plot exists showing next-token probability per layer for at least 3 prompts, and you have written down the layer index where the prediction first becomes top-1 for each.

Day 97 — Linear probe

⏱ ~75 min · Link: Phase 5 guide

Do: Build a probe dataset: run ~50k tokens of Python code through GPT-2-small, label each token "inside a string literal or not," and train sklearn logistic regression from the residual-stream vector at layer L to the label, sweeping L over all 12 layers (background: section 5.2 of the phase guide — and note the caveat: probes show presence, not use).

Explains: Linear probing — high accuracy at layer L means the information is linearly readable from the residual stream there.

Done when: A plot of probe test accuracy vs. layer exists, plus one sentence in your vocabulary notebook naming the peak layer and its accuracy.

Day 98 — A Mathematical Framework, pass 1

⏱ ~90 min · Link: A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits

Do: Read from the start through the one-layer attention-only model: residual stream as a communication bus, attention heads as independent additive paths, and the QK/OV factorization. Skip the two-layer material. Keep yesterday's cached activations open and check each claim against real array shapes (background: section 5.1 of the phase guide).

Explains: The residual-stream formalism — a transformer's output is a sum of paths through heads, and each head splits into a QK circuit (where to attend) and an OV circuit (what to copy).

Done when: Closed notes, you can write the QK circuit (W_Q^T W_K form) and OV circuit (W_O W_V form) matrices with correct shapes on paper in under 10 minutes.

Day 99 — A Mathematical Framework, pass 2

⏱ ~90 min · Link: A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits

Do: Second pass: the two-layer model, head composition (Q-, K-, and V-composition), and virtual attention heads. Work the induction-head sketch at the end slowly — it is the theory behind your Day 100–101 hunt.

Explains: Composition — how two attention heads in different layers chain into a circuit neither implements alone, which is the mechanism behind induction.

Done when: Closed notes, you can write one paragraph correctly distinguishing Q-composition from K-composition and state which one the induction circuit uses.

Day 100 — Induction-head hunt 1: the loss test

⏱ ~60 min · Link: Phase 5 guide

Do: Feed your Phase-2 model a batch of sequences built as 50 random tokens repeated twice (100 tokens total), and plot per-position loss. The second copy should crash. Then open your saved Phase-2 training loss curve and look for the induction phase-change bump (background: section 5.5 item 1 of the phase guide).

Explains: Behavioral evidence of induction — the model copy-pastes from context ("…A B … A → B"), so previously-seen random tokens become predictable.

Done when: A plot exists where mean loss on copy 2 is at least 1.0 nats below copy 1, with both numbers recorded in your vocabulary notebook.

Day 101 — Induction-head hunt 2: localize by ablation

⏱ ~90 min · Link: Phase 5 guide

Do: With hooks, zero-ablate each head's output one at a time and measure the increase in copy-2 loss; rank all heads by damage. For the top candidates, plot their attention pattern on a repeated sequence — a true induction head attends to the token right after the previous occurrence of the current token (background: section 5.2 "Ablation").

Explains: Causal localization — ablation tells you which specific heads carry the induction behavior, not just that the behavior exists.

Done when: You have written down layer.head indices for at least one induction head whose ablation raises copy-2 loss more than any other head's, plus its attention-pattern plot.

Day 103 — Toy Models build 1: the toy model

⏱ ~75 min · Link: Toy Models of Superposition

Do: Read only the article's setup ("Demonstrating Superposition" model definition) — full read is Day 105. In a fresh laptop notebook, implement it: n sparse synthetic features with decaying importance, linear map W down to m < n dims, ReLU readout reconstruction; train with Adam at several sparsity levels (background: section 5.5 item 2 of the phase guide).

Explains: The experimental organism for superposition — a model forced to cram more features than dimensions, where you control sparsity directly.

Done when: Training runs end-to-end and a figure of W^T W heatmaps at 2+ sparsity levels exists, visibly changing with sparsity.

Day 104 — Toy Models build 2: the pentagon

⏱ ~75 min · Link: Toy Models of Superposition

Do: Train the m=2 hidden-dimension variant with 5 equal-importance features at high sparsity and scatter-plot W's five column vectors. Sweep sparsity from dense to sparse and watch the geometry transition from "store only the top 2 features" to a pentagon of 5 superposed directions.

Explains: Superposition made visible — sparse features arrange as near-orthogonal directions at ~72° apart, tolerating interference because they rarely co-occur.

Done when: A saved figure shows the pentagon (5 feature directions, roughly equal angles), reproducing the paper's geometry figure.

Day 105 — Read: Toy Models of Superposition

⏱ ~90 min · Link: Toy Models of Superposition

Do: Full read now that you've built it: the phase diagrams, feature geometry section, and the discussion of polysemanticity and its consequences for interpretability. Skim the appendices.

Explains: Why single neurons are polysemantic and uninterpretable — models store more features than dimensions whenever features are sparse, so directions, not neurons, are the unit of meaning.

Done when: Closed notes, you can write a correct explanation of why sparsity enables superposition, plus a one-line definition of polysemanticity, in under 10 minutes.

Day 106 — SAE build 1: dump the activation dataset

Day 107 — SAE build 2: train it

⏱ ~90 min · Link: Phase 5 guide

Do: Implement the SAE exactly as section 5.3 item 3 of the phase guide describes: encoder (768, 8–16×768), ReLU, decoder with unit-norm columns, loss = reconstruction MSE + λ·L1 on hidden activations. Launch three training runs at low/mid/high λ on yesterday's dump (expected GPU cost ~$5 on a 4090), logging L0 (mean active features per token) and reconstruction MSE for each.

Explains: Dictionary learning against superposition — the sparsity penalty forces the wide hidden layer to find the model's underlying feature directions.

Done when: Three SAE checkpoints are saved to disk, each with a logged (λ, L0, reconstruction-MSE) triple.

Day 109 — SAE build 4: clean features, messy features, λ

⏱ ~90 min · Link: Phase 5 guide

Day 112 — Steering vectors + a skeptical read

⏱ ~75 min · Link: Phase 5 guide

Do: Build a Golden-Gate-style steering vector on GPT-2-small: direction = mean(layer-6 residual activations on 20 prompts about a concept, e.g. weddings) − mean(on 20 neutral prompts). Add α·direction at that layer via hook during generation and sweep α until outputs steer toward the concept without degenerating (background: section 5.2 "Steering"). Last 15 min: skim "An Interpretability Illusion for BERT" (arXiv 2104.07143) — abstract and figures only — the Illusions-genre critique section 5.6 of the phase guide assigns for the skeptical-methodology muscle.

Explains: Features as causal levers — adding a direction to the stream changes behavior, which is intervention-level evidence that directions carry meaning.

Done when: A notebook cell shows 3 steered vs. 3 unsteered generations at your chosen α, at least 2 of the 3 steered outputs mention the concept, and one sentence in your notes states what the BERT interpretability illusion is.
